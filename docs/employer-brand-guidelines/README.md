## Mission

**Solving Problems and Fulfilling Dreams for the Home.**

## Associate Value Proposition pillars

### I have a good job

> "I know Lowe's cares because they invest in me. I'm compensated well, I feel
> taken care of, I have the tools to do my job, and I feel safe in my environment."

- Pay: base pay, bonuses, stock/equity
- Benefits: retirement plan, ESPP, discount, health insurance
- Work: training, tools, role clarity
- Flexibility: schedule, time on/off, location, work/life balance
- Safety: job security and physical safety

### I belong here

> "I love coming to work because of the people around me. My leaders hear me,
> trust me, inspire me and recognize me. I feel like I can be myself around my
> team. And I feel a connection to the impact we make in our communities."

- Meaningful work: serving customers and impact on communities
- Leadership: coaching, being heard, recognition, trust and inspiration
- Sense of community: authenticity, care and compassion, teamwork

### I have a future here

> "I know Lowe's is a place I can plant my roots and create a meaningful career.
> It's a company with 100 years of success, and a history of promoting associates
> of all levels. Here, I can grow my skills to new heights."

- Company direction: history of success, innovation, executive communication
- Career opportunity: career paths, opportunity, internal mobility, promotions
- Personal development: mentorship and internal skills-development programs

## Voice

The voice represents a 100-year company built by millions of people: store
associates sharing practical knowledge, leaders who started in those shoes, and
everyone who has passed or will pass through the gables.

| Tone | Application | Approved direction |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational | Avoid corporate distance; keep it real and relatable. | "Let's make your goals a reality." Avoid slang-heavy forced informality. |
| Inspirational | Spark ambition through real stories and encouragement. | "Every vest tells a story - what will yours be?" Avoid unsupported claims. |
| Confident | Draw on 100 years of experience without arrogance. | "Explore careers to find your fit in a team that grows and wins together." |
| Passionate | Express genuine belief in the rewards of working here. | "We want you to feel welcome and heard, while doing work that makes a difference." |
| Engaging | Use creative devices to hold attention and convey positivity. | "Plant your roots here." Use tasteful wordplay; avoid cheap or pop-culture-dependent puns. |

### Voice do's

- Call Lowe's people **associates**, not team members or employees.
- Be brief; shorter and clearer is better.
- Prefer specific examples, statistics, and testimonials to general claims.
- Find a Lowe's-specific angle rather than copy that could belong to a competitor.

### Voice avoidances

- Do not publish internal terminology externally. For internal audiences, write
  out an acronym the first time it is used.
- Do not stack puns. Keep any pun light and harmless.
- Avoid overplaying prestige. Military communications can use appropriate
  reverence; other audiences should not hear a know-it-all tone.
- Do not be stiff. Natural contractions such as "we're" are acceptable.

### Direct copy and brand voice

- **Direct copy** gives instructions, relays facts, and prompts action. Accuracy
  and clarity are essential. Examples: CTAs, benefit guides, job descriptions,
  and answers to specific questions.
- **Brand voice** captures attention and evokes emotion. It differentiates Lowe's
  in advertising headlines, social copy, welcome cards, and similar materials.

### Associate quotes

Use authentic associate voices whenever possible:

- **To convince:** support a claim, such as a testimonial about a benefit.
- **To inspire:** share an associate's meaningful action or achievement.
- **To connect:** build belonging and community through personal stories.

Look for emotion, uniqueness, specificity, and authenticity. Preserve an
associate's syntax and grammar when practical; heavy editing feels corporate.

## Logo usage

- Use the main Lowe's logo by default. It unites external culture storytelling
  under the company's strongest brand equity.
- A physical careers asset used in a Lowe's store does not need another Lowe's
  logo when the environment already makes the context obvious.
- Internal-team logos may be appropriate for their specific internal events or
  functions and must be paired with the main Lowe's logo.
- Partner/internal marks should be rare and used only in their approved context.
- Never recreate, distort, outline, recolor, or substitute a homemade logo.

## Typography

### Typeface

- Typeface: Fellix by Display Type Foundry
- Released May 2017; updated November 2017
- The guide describes Fellix as airy, geometric, round, bold, friendly, timeless,
  and warm.
- The guide states eight weights / sixteen styles. This repository contains seven
  supplied static weights: Light, Regular, Medium, SemiBold, Bold, ExtraBold, and
  Black. The guide recommends Regular, SemiBold, Bold, and ExtraBold.

### Approved weight formulas

| Formula | Pairing | Use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Regular + Bold | Recommended |
| B | Regular only | Single-weight composition |
| C | Regular + SemiBold | Body and subhead |
| D | SemiBold + ExtraBold | Subhead, headline, and super graphics |

Never pair Bold and ExtraBold as the two contrasting weights in one composition;
the difference is too small to create a clear hierarchy.

### Type hierarchy and spacing

- Fellix Bold is used for H1, SemiBold for H2, and Regular for body copy in the
  demonstrated combined application.
- Space between headline lines is half the headline height.
- Space between subhead and body matches the headline spacing.
- Maximum body-column width in the demonstrated layout is approximately ten words.
- There must be at least a 35% point-size difference between headline and body.
- Type may be placed at the top-left, in a centered area, or bottom-left, while
  the text itself remains left-aligned.
- Minimum margin equals the width of a lowercase `t` set at the headline size.
- Body copy follows the headline's margins and generally left-aligns with it.

### Casing

- Sentence case for longer headlines, subheads, and body copy.
- Title Case for product names and taglines.
- All caps only for short callouts such as CTAs, tags, values, or pricing.
- Never use all caps for full headlines or paragraphs; it reduces legibility.

### Typography don'ts

- Do not center-align, right-align, or justify the text itself.
- Do not orient type vertically, diagonally, or in another non-horizontal direction.
- Do not break words or text blocks into decorative fragments that harm reading.
- Do not use a headline/body size difference smaller than 35%.

### Color-use rules

- Dark Blue should only sit on Blue, White, or Light Blue.
- Blue should only sit on Dark Blue, White, or Light Blue.
- Light Blue should only sit on Dark Blue, White, or Blue.
- Do not place those colors on black or use combinations that remove contrast.
- Lighter backgrounds feel fresh and contemporary; darker backgrounds feel
  luxurious and confident.
- Do not use color merely because it is available; every color needs a reason.

## Visual hierarchy

Use elements in this order of frequency:

1. **Mostly - Gable:** main framing element.
2. **Often - Photography:** realistic imagery and first-choice people content.
3. **Sometimes - Vest + Badge:** brand messaging when photography is unavailable.
4. **Occasionally - Characters:** primarily internal or non-customer-facing.
5. **Rarely - Icons:** ornamental or supporting design elements.

## Gable

The gable is Lowe's recognizable architectural framing device. It can frame,
separate, intertwine, clip, and house content without always showing the full logo.

### Flex gable lockup

- Text and design elements may sit within the flexible gable.
- Parts of the gable may be removed to accommodate text.
- Do not place text around or adjacent to the gable.
- Do not add strokes/outlines to the gable or contained text.

### Intertwining gable

- Intertwine around an associate to add focus and brand recognition.
- Any Lowe's palette color is acceptable when the gable and associate stand out.
- A solid-color falloff can improve separation.
- Never cover an associate's face.
- Avoid background colors that merge with the gable.
- Do not rotate or tilt an intertwining gable.

### Gable as framing device

- Gable and typography may work together when neither obscures the image context.
- The gable may highlight an associate or be used without typography.
- Do not put text on top of the gable line.
- Do not reduce the gable's opacity, stretch it, or alter its geometry.

### Gable clipping mask

- Any image or the entire image content may sit within a gable clip.
- A gable clip may rotate exactly 90 degrees clockwise or counterclockwise.
- Do not use the full logo-bearing gable as an image clipping mask.
- Do not stretch, alter, tilt, or use a slanted gable.

## Photography

### Style

Use authentic, vibrant, sharp imagery with contrasting elements and red/blue
accents. Create humanity and story with props and a lived-in feel without clutter.
Prioritize genuine in-action moments; staged portraits are acceptable when real
associates appear in relevant settings. Match the chosen image to the communication's
emotion and message.

### Portrait and candid uses

- **Portrait:** polished first impressions, professionalism, recruitment,
  inclusive representation, direct response, and targeted campaigns.
- **Candid:** authentic stories, real-world applications, company culture,
  community impact, education, and internal communications.

### Composition

- Use rule-of-thirds placement for dynamic off-center subjects.
- Engage with associates to capture authentic expressions.
- Use framing and negative space to guide attention.
- Avoid clutter, accidental competing focal points, and closed/averted eyes unless
  intentional.
- Creative rule-breaking is acceptable only when balance and harmony remain.

### Cropping

- Preserve enough context to tell the story.
- If a badge matters, frame tightly enough to show its detail without cutting it.
- Keep the entire subject whenever possible.
- Do not crop badges, limbs, faces, joints, products, or essential context.

### Lighting

- Prefer warm, soft, diffused, natural-looking light and saturated color.
- Position the subject toward the light and use varied depth for dimension.
- Avoid harsh midday light, distracting facial shadows, reflections, and glare.
- For community relations, use available natural/ambient light and real moments;
  do not stage, over-light, or over-correct away the authenticity.

### Wardrobe

- Balance aspirational/attainable presentation with authenticity to the task.
- In-store associates always wear a clean, serviceable, zipped vest.
- Prefer neutral colors and classic patterns, but use contrast to separate the
  subject from the background.
- Do not show outside brand names or logos unless authorized.
- Follow the Associate Handbook dress code.

### Retouching

- Preserve real humans, natural skin, real texture, and natural light.
- Enhance rather than alter.
- Avoid excessive smoothing, airbrushing, sharpening, contrast, filters, and
  color casts.

### Environment

- Use flattering natural light and backgrounds with useful texture.
- Choose a location that supports the subject and make the person comfortable.
- Avoid clutter, distracting background objects, artificial-only lighting when
  natural light is available, and objects that appear to grow from the subject.

### Photography checklist

- Organic movement and authentic poses
- Representation across ages, abilities, body shapes, genders, races,
  ethnicities, professions, and nationalities
- Uncluttered environments
- Soft, natural-looking light and warm balanced colors
- No unauthorized third-party logos
- One clear focal point with room for crops and overlays
- No lens flare, flash-heavy treatment, harsh studio shadows, or elaborate effects

### Hi-fi versus lo-fi

- **Hi-fi:** high-resolution, crisp, generally professional and staged. Default
  for most assets when professionalism and sharpness are priorities.
- **Lo-fi:** lower-resolution associate-captured content. Use primarily for social
  media and campaigns where authenticity is central.

## Vest and badge

- Associates' vests are a distinctive point of view and rich visual device.
- Red vest is the default. Military or MST vests are used for communications
  specific to those groups.
- When tightly cropped, show the side with the Lowe's logo for recognition.
- Vests may carry multiple badges and a customized name/role box.
- Do not overlay unrelated text/graphics on a vest, distort it, or let it merge
  into the background.
- In multi-vest layouts, place the red vest first by default; a group-specific
  vest may lead in a targeted communication.
- Layer multiple vests intentionally; the demonstrated optimal rotation is 21°.
- Badges always sit on top of a vest. Multiple badges may be grouped on either
  side when aesthetically balanced.
- Do not overlay text on a badge, stretch/squeeze badges, or overlap badges.

Badge categories include language, tenure/years of service, Service Star Award,
and miscellaneous achievements, skills, and initiative participation.

The vest colors and their exact printed specifications are shown on page 49.

## Characters

Characters are a fallback when the right photo is unavailable and offer flexible,
inclusive storytelling. They are not the first choice for external/customer-facing
work.

- Character work must be created or modified by Lowe's designers through the
  Creative Services Team.
- A gender-neutral base head supports diverse features and expressions.
- Hairstyles and facial hair should reflect race, age, identity, and personality.
- Designers may add inclusive hair/facial-hair variants to the asset library.
- Figures are approximately nine heads tall, intentionally graphical rather than
  strictly realistic.
- Poses communicate narrative, attitude, and intention; variation is encouraged.
- Military figures are illustrative and do not represent actual uniforms or poses.
- Diverse-ability references include mobility aids and visible/non-visible
  variations shown in the source.

## Design elements and iconography

- Culture of Winning iconography maps each core behavior and value to a visual.
- Two icon types are supported: outlined and filled.
- Icons support navigation, interaction, and clear communication.
- Never pinch, stretch, rotate, recolor, change stroke width, add effects,
  reflections, or shadows, or fill outlined icons arbitrarily.
- Product illustrations are functional visual-communication tools and help build
  cohesive platform-specific stories.
- Illustrated scenarios may be tailored to the audience or used as aesthetic
  enhancements.
